Forensic Narrative Infrastructure

Market manipulation has moved into the information layer.

Market Prism is a forensic narrative engine. It anchors every market story to the SEC filing it claims to be about, scores the credibility of its sources, and detects when a narrative is being coordinated — upstream of the price action it is engineered to create.

The problem

Sentiment is being manufactured upstream of price.

For most of the last decade, market-sentiment analysis meant counting bullish versus bearish keywords. Generative AI ended that. A single actor can now produce thousands of polished, on-message articles, posts, and “analysis” pages in an afternoon — and the open web has crossed into structural saturation, where low-quality sites compete to repeat whatever talking point was profitable yesterday.

The consequence is an information environment in which narratives can be engineered, amplified, and coordinated faster than oversight built on trade and order-flow data alone can detect. Sentiment is increasingly manufactured upstream of price. Individual investors are the most exposed — but the integrity of price formation is the broader casualty.

Market Prism was built to make that manufactured layer visible, and to show its work all the way down to the filing each narrative claims to track.

How it works

Forensic, not predictive.

Market Prism is not an LLM wrapper or another sentiment dashboard. It is a seven-step forensic pipeline that anchors every narrative claim to a primary source, weighs the credibility of who is making it, and detects when a story is being coordinated rather than independently formed. Three principles separate it from the standard sentiment stack:

  1. 01
    SEC-anchored. Every claim is checked against the actual 10-K, 10-Q, or 8-K filing. The drift between a narrative and the filing it cites is the first place we look.
  2. 02
    Source-authority weighted. A single post from a verified domain expert can carry more narrative weight than a thousand polished, synthetically generated articles. We score the source, not the volume.
  3. 03
    Coordination-aware. When several outlets publish near-identical framing inside a narrow window, that is not consensus — it is a campaign signature. Market Prism flags it.

The full pipeline is patent-pending across three filings. The objective is straightforward: give any analyst the kind of forensic read on a market narrative that, until recently, only an institutional research desk could produce.

The thesis

In 2026, the noise is the signal.

Market Prism treats the noise of the saturated web as a roadmap rather than a problem to suppress. Nearly every market move begins as a story told in a niche corner of the internet. It migrates to the mainstream. Then it reaches saturation — the point at which every low-quality site on the first page of search results is repeating the same line. That moment of saturation is, more often than not, the moment price action reverses.

We call it narrative exhaustion, and identifying it is the core of the system. Market Prism converts an information environment that overwhelms most analysts into a measurable, contrarian indicator.

Under the hood

Machine learning, with fuel rates per ticker.

Beneath the surface, Market Prism is a machine-learning system trained on the historical relationship between narrative state and price outcome. It learns, ticker by ticker, which kinds of stories precede which kinds of moves — and the projections it surfaces (1D, 5D, 30D, and 90D move bands) are model output, not heuristic rules.

Every ticker is also assigned its own narrative fuel rate — a physics-style coefficient describing how quickly a story burns through market attention. A megacap does not process narrative the way a small-cap biotech does: large names have deep reserves and slow combustion; smaller names ignite and exhaust quickly. The fuel rate is what lets the model say “this story has roughly six trading days of runway left” instead of merely “sentiment is bullish.”

Together they produce something the standard data stack cannot replicate: a per-ticker, ML-driven, filing-anchored projection of where a narrative is heading and how long it has before it runs out of fuel.
